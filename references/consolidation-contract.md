# Student-led consolidation contract

Use this stage only when the completed task has enough learning value to justify retrieval and reflection. Keep it short for modest tasks and skip it for trivial corrections.

## Natural sequence

1. Ask the learner to reconstruct the route without rereading the solution when practical.
2. Let the learner produce as much as possible before correcting.
3. Check causal order, missing steps, boundaries, and the reason behind key decisions.
4. Cue an omission before supplying it.
5. Correct a misconception or overgeneralization immediately.
6. Extract a transferable pattern only when the task supports one.
7. Identify the learner's personal bottleneck.
8. Form a concise final version using the learner's accurate wording where possible.
9. Update evidence-scoped Student State.

Use one prompt at a time when reconstruction is interactive. Do not dump a questionnaire.

## Scaffolding when recall fails

Escalate minimally:

- ask for the first decision;
- provide the beginning of a sentence;
- name the representation or subproblem that came first;
- offer two plausible next steps and ask the learner to distinguish them;
- supply the missing step only after lighter cues fail.

The scaffold itself changes the evidence: a correct answer after a strong cue supports `Developing`, not independent demonstration.

## Three useful outputs

Collect only what the task warrants:

- **Problem-specific summary:** the route used in this task.
- **Transferable strategy:** the clue, action, and boundary that may help on related tasks, including why it works.
- **Personal learning point:** what the learner actually lacked or misread this time.

A transferable pattern should normally have the form:

`observable clue -> useful move -> reason -> boundary or exception`

Do not turn a one-off trick into a universal rule.

## Transfer value

Infer silently:

- **High:** the recognition pattern applies broadly and rests on a stable principle.
- **Medium:** the method is reusable within a bounded problem family.
- **Low:** the move depends on a special construction, numbers, or one-off trick.

For Low transfer, state the narrow lesson or omit transfer entirely.

## Evidence outcomes

- **Independent and accurate:** mark the relevant item `Demonstrated in this episode`.
- **Accurate after light cueing:** mark it `Developing`, recording the cue.
- **Procedure recalled without why:** keep the causal skill `Developing`.
- **Answer known but route unavailable:** do not infer method mastery; rebuild the problem structure.
- **Critical step omitted:** cue the missing transition, then reassess.
- **False general rule:** correct the rule and establish its boundary.

These outcomes update the current episode only. Do not silently promote them into a durable mastery model.

## Behavioral regression cases

- **A — Independent summary:** reduce help and only correct material inaccuracies.
- **B — Answer without method:** rebuild the solving structure; do not accept completion as mastery.
- **C — Missing key step:** cue the location of the gap before revealing it.
- **D — Overgeneralization:** correct the false universal rule and supply its boundary.
- **E — High transfer:** extract a reusable clue-action-reason pattern.
- **F — Low transfer:** avoid a universal claim and keep the conclusion narrow.
- **G — Former missing skill demonstrated:** update that item to `Demonstrated in this episode` with evidence.
